Chinese reader

Paste any Chinese text. It is cut into words, each word is set over its pinyin and its meaning, and hovering one opens its dictionary entry. Click the words you want to keep and export them as a vocabulary list. Everything runs in your browser — the text you paste never leaves it.

    How the text is cut into words

    Chinese is written without spaces, so before anything can be looked up the text has to be split. This page does it with the dictionary itself: among all the ways of covering a run of characters with dictionary headwords, it takes the one with the highest total corpus probability, computed by dynamic programming over the run. Frequency counts come from jieba's table; the vocabulary is CC-CEDICT and nothing else.

    Restricting the vocabulary that way costs something — a name or a word coined last year will be broken into single characters — but it buys a property worth more here: every word the reader shows you has an entry behind it, so a hover never comes up empty. Characters that no headword covers are shown on their own and looked up individually.

    Splitting into words first is also what makes the readings correct. 行 is xíng in 行走 and háng in 银行; a character-by-character annotator has to guess, while a word-based one simply reads the entry. What it cannot settle is a lone character doing grammatical work — 地 is dì, "earth", and also the neutral de that marks an adverb, and only the surrounding grammar says which. Where a character has more than one reading, the line under it shows the commonest and the card lists them all.

    The dictionary

    Definitions and readings come from CC-CEDICT, about 119,000 entries, used under CC BY-SA. It ships as a single compressed file of roughly 3.6 MB, downloaded the first time you annotate something and then cached, so the rest of the site and the first visit to this page cost nothing. Both simplified and traditional text are accepted.
